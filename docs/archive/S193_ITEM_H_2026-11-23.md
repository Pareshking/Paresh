## The stock picker move

- **What a Streamlit range click redoes** (`stock_chart.price_chart_panes`): the whole stock page reruns (measured 2.5-2.9 s a click, RELIANCE and HAPPYFORGE, settle included). The range itself changes four things:
  - the close is cut to the window (`window`);
  - the 50D EMA is computed once on the full history and only cut, so the range does not change it;
  - volume is cut, and each bar's colour comes from the close against the previous close inside the window (so the first bar's colour can differ between ranges);
  - **RS is rebased to 100 at the window's first session** (every value changes: RELIANCE latest 94.8 / 88.2 / 85.4 for 6M / 12M / 3Y).

  `build_panes` also picks candles or a line from the window, but the stock page passes no high or low, so it is always a line. The `sr` summary and the axis tick format (`SPAN_DAYS`) follow the range.
- **Can the frame hold every range at once:** yes. 6M and 12M are subsets of 3Y. The spec is 51 KB at 12M (frame 71 KB) and 131 KB at 3Y (RELIANCE 746 sessions; frame 151 KB). Send the 3Y close, the EMA, the volume with its colours, and the market close (or the stock-over-market ratio), about 135-140 KB per stock, once (+80 KB over today). In JS: on a chip, `setData` with the slice, RS = ratio ÷ ratio[first] × 100, then fit content, move the end dots and repaint the legend. A switch then takes no rerun. Size could drop about 20% by rounding to 2 dp and sending the volume colours as a bit string.
- **Estimate:** about half a day. That covers chips in `lw_chart` (opt-in spec field `ranges` plus `default`, about 60 lines of JS), `price_chart_panes` returning one 3Y spec (about 30 lines), removing `st.segmented_control`, updating `tests/test_stock_page_v2.py`, and rerunning `chart_hover_audit.mjs`, `layout_audit` and `check_dashboard_requirements` on the stock page. Risk: STOCK_REQUIREMENTS row 33's check looks for "6M + 12M + 3Y". If `check_dashboard_requirements.mjs` reads only the main document, the chips inside the frame will fail it (not checked). Market M5 and M6 are a second, larger move: `compute_*` runs with `lookback=history_days`, so chips need the 12M (or 3Y) series computed once. That compute cost was not measured.

## Proposed check (`scripts/layout_audit.mjs`, in the frame probe; it already runs on every page in CI)

1. **The spec states its default.** Every time-range chart (`breadth`, `lines`, `bars` with dates, `growth`, `btgrowth`, `rel`, `pairs`, LW) carries `range: {default: "12M"|"all"|"<n>", why: "lately"|"record"|"fixed"}`. The kit writes `data-range-default`, `data-range-from`, `data-range-to` and `data-range-first` (the data's first date) on `#c` or `#root` after it draws. Add a pytest that `dash_charts.frame_html` and `lw_chart.chart_html` raise on a time-range kind without it, and that `lately` maps to 12M and `record` to all.
2. **Opens on its stated range.** In the frame: the pressed chip's label equals the default, if there are chips. "12M": the first drawn date lies within ±7 days of last − 12 months, or equals the data's first date when the data is shorter. "all": the first drawn date equals the data's first date. Otherwise fail with `chart opens off its stated range`.
3. **Readout at rest.** Before any pointer or key: every `.readout`, `.ph` (dash) or `.lg` (LW) of a chart frame is non-empty and holds a digit; LW's first pane also holds a date. Otherwise fail with `chart has no readout at rest`. Exempt: `holdings`, `history`, `stable` (tables), and images or sparklines in tables (C7).
4. **One picker.** Fail if the main document has an `stButtonGroup` whose options all match `^(\d+[MY]|All)$` (catches St1 and Market's Period today).
5. Optional: extend `chart_hover_audit.mjs` from LW to dash `.chart` frames (the readout changes under the pointer and on ArrowLeft, and the plot does not move). `inv.mjs` already does this in about 20 lines.

Cost: about 40 lines in `layout_audit.mjs` and a few seconds a page. No new job. CI runs it on 750 only, which is enough: defaults do not depend on the system, and the pytest covers the spec.
